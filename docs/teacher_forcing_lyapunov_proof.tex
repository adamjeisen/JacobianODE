\subsection{Adaptive teacher-forcing rate as a Lyapunov-driven curriculum}
\label{sec:tf-lyap}

We show that the teacher-forcing rate $\alpha$ used during training,
computed online from the leading discrete-time Lyapunov exponent of the
\emph{learned} Jacobian field, equals the critical rate derived by
\citet{HessEtAl2023} for bounded error propagation under generalized
teacher forcing. The substitution of the true Jacobian by the learned
one yields an adaptive curriculum: $\alpha$ is maximal early in
training, when predictions are unstable, and contracts toward the
intrinsic critical rate of the underlying dynamics as the model
converges.

\paragraph{Setup.}
Let the (unknown) ground-truth dynamics be $x_{t+1}^\star = F_\star(x_t^\star)$
and the learned model dynamics be $z_{t+1} = F(z_t)$, with $F$ the
one-step flow induced by integrating the learned continuous-time
Jacobian field $A_\theta(z)$ over a step $\Delta t$. We do not assume
$F = F_\star$; the residual $\delta_t := F(x_t^\star) - F_\star(x_t^\star)$
is the irreducible one-step model error at the true state and shrinks
as $\theta$ trains.
The corresponding one-step Jacobian along a trajectory is
%
\begin{equation}
  J_t \;:=\; \left.\partial F/\partial z\right|_{z_t}
  \;=\; \exp\!\bigl(A(z_t)\,\Delta t\bigr).
  \label{eq:Jt}
\end{equation}
%
The matrix exponential in \eqref{eq:Jt} is exactly the operation
applied to predicted Jacobians inside the training loop before passing
them to the Lyapunov estimator (see Sec.~\ref{sec:implementation}).

The discrete-time Lyapunov spectrum along a trajectory is
%
\begin{equation}
  \lambda_i \;:=\; \lim_{T \to \infty}
  \frac{1}{T}\,\log\sigma_i\!\Bigl(\prod_{t=0}^{T-1} J_t\Bigr),
  \quad i=1,\dots,d,
  \label{eq:lyap-spec}
\end{equation}
%
with $\sigma_i$ singular values sorted in descending order, so that
$\lambda_1 \equiv \lambda_{\max}$ governs the asymptotic growth of
generic perturbations: $\|\delta z_T\| \sim \|\delta z_0\|\,e^{T\lambda_{\max}}$.

\paragraph{Generalized teacher forcing.}
In rollout training, the model produces a sequence $\hat{x}_t$ to be
matched against ground truth $x_t^\star$. Generalized teacher forcing
\citep{HessEtAl2023} feeds the network a convex blend of prediction
and ground truth at each step:
%
\begin{equation}
  \hat{x}_{t+1} \;=\; F\!\bigl((1-\alpha)\,\hat{x}_t + \alpha\, x_t^\star\bigr),
  \qquad \alpha \in [0,1].
  \label{eq:gtf}
\end{equation}
%
$\alpha = 1$ recovers hard teacher forcing; $\alpha = 0$ recovers
free-running rollout.

\paragraph{Linearized error recursion.}
Define the prediction error $e_t := \hat{x}_t - x_t^\star$.
Substituting $\hat{x}_t = x_t^\star + e_t$ into the convex blend
inside $F$ in \eqref{eq:gtf} rewrites the argument as a perturbation
of the true state:
%
\begin{equation}
  (1-\alpha)\,\hat{x}_t + \alpha\,x_t^\star
  \;=\; (1-\alpha)(x_t^\star + e_t) + \alpha\,x_t^\star
  \;=\; x_t^\star + (1-\alpha)\,e_t,
  \label{eq:blend-rewrite}
\end{equation}
%
so $\hat{x}_{t+1} = F\bigl(x_t^\star + (1-\alpha)\,e_t\bigr)$.
Bridging through $F(x_t^\star)$ and using
$x_{t+1}^\star = F_\star(x_t^\star)$,
%
\begin{align}
  e_{t+1}
  &= F\!\bigl(x_t^\star + (1-\alpha)\,e_t\bigr) - F_\star(x_t^\star)
    \nonumber \\
  &= \underbrace{\bigl[F\!\bigl(x_t^\star + (1-\alpha)\,e_t\bigr)
    - F(x_t^\star)\bigr]}_{\text{GTF perturbation, linearizable}}
    \;+\;
    \underbrace{\bigl[F(x_t^\star) - F_\star(x_t^\star)\bigr]}_{=\,\delta_t}
    \nonumber \\
  &= (1-\alpha)\,J_t\,e_t \;+\; \delta_t \;+\; \mathcal{O}\!\bigl(\|e_t\|^2\bigr).
  \label{eq:error-recursion}
\end{align}
%
Here $J_t = \partial F/\partial z|_{x_t^\star}$ is the \emph{learned}
Jacobian at the \emph{true} state, and $\delta_t$ is the one-step
model residual at $x_t^\star$.
%
Writing the linearized propagator as
$\Phi_{s\to T} := \prod_{t=s}^{T-1} (1-\alpha)\,J_t$ and unrolling
\eqref{eq:error-recursion} from $e_0 = 0$ (rollout matched to truth at
the start),
%
\begin{equation}
  e_T \;\approx\; \Phi_{0\to T}\,e_0
  \;+\; \sum_{s=0}^{T-1} \Phi_{s+1\to T}\,\delta_s.
  \label{eq:error-product}
\end{equation}
%
The first term is initial-condition transport; the second is an
integral of model residual driven through the same propagator. Both
contributions decay/grow at the same rate.

\paragraph{Stability bound.}
By \eqref{eq:lyap-spec}, the operator norm of the linearized
propagator satisfies
$\|\Phi_{s\to T}\|^{1/(T-s)} \to (1-\alpha)\,e^{\lambda_{\max}}$ as
$T-s \to \infty$. Three regimes for $e_T$ emerge depending on
$\rho := (1-\alpha)\,e^{\lambda_{\max}}$:
%
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\rho < 1$: \eqref{eq:error-product} is a geometric series in
  $\delta_s$. The transient $\Phi_{0\to T}\,e_0$ decays exponentially,
  and the residual-driven sum is bounded by
  $\sup_s\|\delta_s\|/(1-\rho)$. Trajectory remains bounded for all
  $T$, with steady-state error proportional to model residual.
  \item $\rho = 1$: the transient is constant; the driven sum grows
  like $\sqrt{T}\,\sigma_\delta$ (random-walk regime). Marginal.
  \item $\rho > 1$: both terms grow exponentially; catastrophic
  blow-up.
\end{itemize}
%
Bounded error propagation in the linear regime therefore requires
%
\begin{equation}
  (1 - \alpha)\,e^{\lambda_{\max}} \;\le\; 1
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \alpha \;\ge\; 1 - e^{-\lambda_{\max}}.
  \label{eq:critical-bound}
\end{equation}
%
Define the \emph{critical teacher-forcing rate}
%
\begin{equation}
  \boxed{\;\alpha^\star \;:=\; \max\!\bigl(0,\;1 - e^{-\lambda_{\max}}\bigr).\;}
  \label{eq:alpha-star}
\end{equation}
%
For chaotic dynamics ($\lambda_{\max} > 0$) one needs strictly
positive teacher forcing; for contracting dynamics
($\lambda_{\max} \le 0$) the error decays on its own and $\alpha^\star = 0$.
This recovers \citet[Theorem~1]{HessEtAl2023}.

\paragraph{Adaptive evaluation on the learned Jacobians.}
Hess et al.'s bound is a statement about the \emph{true}
$\lambda_{\max}$, which is unknown a priori. We replace it by the
leading Lyapunov exponent of the \emph{learned} dynamics, evaluated
online during training using the predicted Jacobians along the current
trajectory:
%
\begin{equation}
  \widehat{\lambda}_{\max}^{\,(\theta)}
  \;:=\;
  \frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=0}^{T-1} \log r_t^{(1)},
  \quad
  r_t^{(1)} := R_{tt}\bigl(\mathrm{QR}\!\bigl(J_t\,Q_{t-1}\bigr)\bigr)_{1,1},
  \label{eq:lyap-online}
\end{equation}
%
where $J_t = \exp(A_\theta(z_t)\Delta t)$ and the $Q_t$ are the
QR-orthogonalized basis updates from the standard Bennetin-style
estimator. The online teacher-forcing rate is then
%
\begin{equation}
  \alpha^{(\theta)} \;:=\; \max\!\bigl(0,\;1 - e^{-\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}^{\,(\theta)}}\bigr),
  \label{eq:alpha-theta}
\end{equation}
%
which is the \emph{exact} substitution of $\alpha^\star$ in
\eqref{eq:alpha-star} with the learned Jacobian field. There is no
approximation: \eqref{eq:alpha-theta} would equal Hess et al.'s
$\alpha^\star$ if the learned dynamics matched the truth.

\paragraph{Curriculum interpretation.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:alpha-theta} defines an adaptive curriculum, with
three regimes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Early training.} $A_\theta$ is near random
  initialization; predicted trajectories are unstable and
  $\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}^{\,(\theta)}$ is large, driving
  $\alpha^{(\theta)} \to 1$. The error recursion
  \eqref{eq:error-recursion} contracts to
  $e_{t+1} \approx 0$, so training is dominated by hard teacher
  forcing and the error does not propagate even though the model
  is far from accurate.
  \item \emph{Mid training.} As $A_\theta$ approaches the true
  Jacobian field, $\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}^{\,(\theta)} \to
  \lambda_{\max}^{\,(\text{true})}$ and $\alpha^{(\theta)}$
  decreases monotonically (in expectation) to the system's
  intrinsic critical rate.
  \item \emph{Convergence.} $\alpha^{(\theta)} \to \alpha^\star$,
  the smallest value of $\alpha$ for which trajectory generation
  is provably bounded under the true dynamics. The model is now
  rolled out almost free-running, with teacher forcing applied
  only to the extent dictated by the true Lyapunov spectrum.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Coupling to the learning-rate schedule.}
The learning rate is set in this work by
%
\begin{equation}
  \eta_t \;=\; \eta_{\min} + s\!\bigl(\alpha_t\bigr)\,(\eta_{\max} - \eta_{\min}),
  \quad
  s(\alpha) := \frac{\alpha}{\alpha + (1-\alpha)\,e^{-k\alpha}},
  \label{eq:lr-schedule}
\end{equation}
%
with sharpness $k=1$ matching the schedule used in the original
\textsc{JacobianODE} work \citep{TODOOriginalJacobianODE}. By
\eqref{eq:alpha-theta} and \eqref{eq:lr-schedule}, both the
teacher-forcing rate and the learning rate are governed by the same
Lyapunov-derived clock $\widehat{\lambda}_{\max}^{\,(\theta)}$: when
the predictor is unstable ($\alpha$ large), $\eta$ is large and
training takes aggressive steps under strong teacher correction; once
the predictor stabilizes ($\alpha \to \alpha^\star$), $\eta$
contracts to $\eta_{\min}$ and training enters a fine-tuning regime.
This eliminates the need for a separate, epoch-indexed schedule (e.g.,
$T_{\max}$ in cosine annealing) and yields a curriculum whose pace is
determined by the data rather than by wall-clock training duration.
